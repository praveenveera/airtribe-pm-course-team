# Evidence register

## Reading guide

- Official rules and government data receive the highest weight for policy facts.
- Company pages are valid evidence of what the company announced or claims, not proof that targets were delivered.
- Consultant reports provide useful estimates and survey data, but their methodology, age, and possible sponsor interests limit generalisation.
- News reporting is used for recent enforcement and company statements when primary filings are unavailable.

## Sources

### S01 — Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines 2025

- **Type/date:** Government of India / 2025.
- **Key fact:** Clause 23 says state governments *may* allow aggregators to aggregate non-transport motorcycles for passenger journeys. State permission remains necessary.
- **Limitation:** National guidelines do not confirm any state licence or live operational permission.
- **URL:** [Parivahan — Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines 2025](https://parivahan.gov.in/sites/default/files/NOTIFICATION%26ADVISORY/MV-Aggregators-Guidelines-2025%20-%20English%20and%20Hindi.pdf)

### S02 — Delhi Motor Vehicle Aggregator and Delivery Service Provider Scheme, 2023

- **Type/date:** Government of NCT Delhi / 2023.
- **Key fact:** Bike taxis are allowed subject to compliance; vehicles newly onboarded from scheme commencement must be electric and low-speed EVs are excluded.
- **Limitation:** A published scheme does not prove a specific company currently holds every required licence or permit.
- **URL:** [Delhi Transport Department — scheme PDF](https://transport.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/Transport/generic_multiple_files/policy.pdf)

### S03 — Maharashtra Bike-Taxi Rules, 2025

- **Key facts:** Proposed licensing structure; minimum 50 electric bike taxis in ownership/agreement; state registration, fitness, insurance, parking/maintenance/charging and control-room requirements.

### S05 — KPMG: Unlocking the potential of bike-taxis in India

- **Type/date:** KPMG India report / March 2024.
- **Key facts:** Estimated 280m rides across three aggregators in 2022; average trip around 6 km; ~50% of trips 4-8 km; 2,638-driver survey across nine cities; driver earnings, work patterns, multi-homing, and EV concerns; historical fares ₹8-10/km.
- **Limitations:** Much of the field data is from 2023 or earlier. The report is advocacy-oriented toward enabling bike taxis and should not substitute for current Uber data.
- **URL:** [KPMG report PDF](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2024/03/unlocking-the-potential-of-bike-taxis-in-india.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)

### S06 — CEEW vehicle total cost of ownership study

- **Type/date:** Independent policy research / 2025.
- **Key fact:** National-average TCO for two-wheelers was estimated at ₹1.48/km electric versus ₹2.46/km petrol; EV economics improve with higher utilisation.
- **Limitations:** Model uses representative assumptions and does not include each Uber partner’s lease, downtime, incentive, or city-specific cash flow.
- **URL:** [CEEW — What Drives Vehicle Ownership Costs in India?](https://www.ceew.in/publications/cost-of-ownership-for-road-transport-sector-for-different-vehicle-segments-fuels-and-powertrains)

## Contradictions and unresolved points

| Topic | Evidence conflict / gap | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| PM E-DRIVE e-2W terminal date | July 2026 PIB answer cited 31 July 2026; August notification register shows a later extension/update | Treat August register as newer; obtain signed amendment for exact terms |
| Rapido scale | 3m daily rides in Feb 2025 versus >5m reported Sep 2026 | Both are company claims across categories; use as trend, not audited market share |
| Ola 10k e-Bikes | Announced for 2024; current delivery not found | Mark unverified, do not count as active supply |
| Uber-Zypp 10k EVs | Announced for 2024; only 1k deployment directly verified in reviewed public sources | Mark unverified, request internal fleet data |
| Maharashtra | Electric bike-taxi rules existed, then provisional licences were revoked | Current enforcement decision overrides launch assumptions; no-go pending written change |
| Karnataka | Strong historic demand, but court/state permission has shifted | Demand does not equal legal permission; no-go pending current rules |

## Evidence still required for an investment case

1. Current signed state orders and Uber licences.
2. Uber city/category financial and marketplace data.
3. Vehicle-by-vehicle EV operating records.
4. Partner audited capacity, financial health, related-party exposure, and contingency plan.
5. Fresh rider and driver research.
6. Current normalised fares and driver/platform charges across competitors in matched routes.
7. App-based mystery shopping across target cities and times.
8. Safety incident data per million trips.
