# Decision memo

## Decision

**Proceed with a 12-week, partner-led electric Uber Bike pilot in Delhi, subject to regulatory and operating-readiness gates. Do not approve a nationwide EV-only mandate, a separate standalone app, or an Uber-owned two-wheeler fleet.**

## Decision frame

| Item | Definition |
|---|---|
| Primary rider | Price-sensitive urban commuter making a 3-8 km first/last-mile or point-to-point trip |
| Rider job | “Get me to work, transit, college, or an errand quickly and affordably.” |
| Driver | Existing or prospective bike-taxi partner, often multi-homing across platforms |
| Driver job | “Help me reach my daily earning target without new debt or lost working time.” |
| Business outcome | Grow completed utility trips and driver supply while preserving contribution margin and regulatory permission |
| Pilot north-star | **Completed EV-bike trips per online EV-driver hour** |
| Guardrails | Driver net earnings/hour, ETA, completion, cancellation, safety, downtime, support cost, and contribution/trip |

## Evidence supporting a “go”

1. **FACT — proven use case.** KPMG estimated approximately 280 million bike-taxi rides across three aggregators in 2022. Its 2023 survey of 2,638 bike-taxi drivers found an average trip of about 6 km, with roughly half of trips at 4-8 km and frequent first/last-mile use. [S05]
2. **FACT — category growth.** Redseer’s February 2026 analysis says incremental ride-hailing growth is increasingly driven by autos and two-wheelers, especially in non-metro markets. [S10]
3. **FACT — attractive lifecycle economics.** CEEW estimated national-average two-wheeler total cost of ownership at ₹1.48/km for electric and ₹2.46/km for petrol, while noting infrastructure, reliability, and upfront-cost constraints. [S06]
4. **FACT — enabling market.** India sold 1,149,334 electric two-wheelers in FY2024-25, 21% above FY2023-24. [S07]
5. **VENDOR CLAIM — demonstrated competitor demand.** Ola said its Bengaluru e-bike pilot completed more than 1.75 million rides, expanded the category by 40% within three months, and used 200 charging stations. [S17]
6. **FACT/VENDOR CLAIM — Uber already has a base.** Uber currently markets Uber Bike in India and promoted ₹25 rides for up to 3 km in April 2026. In 2023 it reported 1,000 Zypp e-two-wheelers deployed on Uber Moto in Delhi and announced a 10,000-vehicle target. [S13][S14][S21]

## Evidence against a broad launch

1. **FACT — permission is state-specific and unstable.** The 2025 national aggregator guidelines allow states to permit private motorcycles; they do not create automatic nationwide permission. Delhi allows only electric vehicles for newly onboarded bike taxis under its 2023 scheme. Maharashtra revoked provisional licences for Ola, Uber, and Rapido in March 2026. Karnataka operations have faced court-ordered suspension pending state rules. [S01][S02][S04][S24]
2. **FACT — driver adoption is not automatic.** In KPMG’s 2023 driver survey, 100% used an existing motorcycle. Reported concerns with an EV-only rule included not needing another vehicle (66.7%), high upfront cost (45.2%), and range/weight capability (38.9%). [S05]
3. **VENDOR CLAIM — a scaled bike-first rival leads.** Rapido said it held 70% of India’s bike-taxi market in April 2025; a September 2026 profile reported company claims of more than 5 million daily rides across categories and presence in more than 400 cities. [S12]
4. **FACT — low-ticket economics are operationally demanding.** Redseer says two-wheelers remain commission-based because of low ticket sizes and elastic supply, while utility-led growth is price-sensitive and operationally intensive. [S10]
5. **UNKNOWN — Uber’s own proof is incomplete publicly.** The reviewed sources do not disclose Uber Bike city economics or confirm delivery and utilisation of the announced 10,000 Zypp vehicles.

## Strategic choice

| Option | Customer value | Learning speed | Capital risk | Regulatory risk | Decision |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A. National EV-only Uber Bike | High if supply exists | Medium | High | Very high | **Reject now** |
| B. Partner-led, city-gated pilot | High in selected zones | High | Low-medium | Containable | **Recommend** |
| C. EV badge only for existing supply | Modest | Medium | Low | Medium | Use inside pilot, not as the strategy |
| D. Wait and observe | None | Low | Low | Low | Keep as fallback if gates fail |
| E. Uber-owned EV fleet | Potential supply control | Medium | Very high | High | **Reject** |

## Recommendation

Use Uber’s existing Bike product, demand base, safety stack, and fleet-partner model. Add a visible **Electric** badge and preferential EV matching within controlled Delhi zones. Do not split a thin supply pool into a separate app or force riders into a longer wait merely to receive an EV.

The differentiated promise is not “green rides” alone:

> **The fastest affordable last-mile ride, with an electric vehicle that does not reduce driver earnings or trip reliability.**

## Conditions for approval

All must be green before public launch:

- Written confirmation of aggregator licence, vehicle permit/registration, insurance, driver, helmet, and operating-area requirements.
- Geo-fencing that prevents an authorised Delhi trip from silently becoming an unauthorised cross-border NCR operation.
- At least two fleet/vehicle or energy partners; no single-partner dependency for vehicles, batteries, charging, or maintenance.
- Verified real-world range under pillion load and hot-weather stop/start use.
- Maintenance and battery-swap service-level agreements, with replacement-vehicle and outage plans.
- Driver proposition showing expected **net earnings per online hour** after all EV costs—not fuel savings alone.
- Safety and incident-response review for rider helmets, vehicle condition, night operations, and women riders/drivers.
- Finance, legal, operations, safety, and city-team sign-off.

## What would change the recommendation?

- Choose **wait/observe** if licensing remains ambiguous, partner uptime cannot be contracted, or driver net earnings trail the comparable petrol cohort.
- Choose a faster multi-city rollout if two independent cities show equal-or-better driver earnings, non-inferior service reliability, positive unit economics, and stable written regulation.
- Consider dedicated EV supply ownership only if multiple partners fail repeatedly and a full lifecycle model proves ownership beats guaranteed third-party capacity after financing, residual-value, maintenance, and governance risk. Current evidence does not support that move.

## Confidence

**Medium on strategic direction; low on investment size and rollout timing.** Public data supports the use case and EV economics, but internal platform economics, current EV supply, and live licences are missing.
