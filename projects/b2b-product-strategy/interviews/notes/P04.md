## Recent case

### 1. Last difficult customer-support issue

A customer reported that a workflow stopped working correctly after a product update. The problem did not affect every account, so support needed to determine whether the cause was customer configuration, permissions or a product change. The customer's administrator and Customer Success Manager became involved.

### 2. Systems and people involved

`Zendesk intake → ticket and account review → Salesforce and internal-tool checks → customer evidence request → reproduction attempt → Jira escalation → engineering request for timestamp and request ID → customer reproduction → workaround → product fix → customer confirmation`

People: Frontline practitioner, customer administrator, Customer Success Manager, another support agent and engineering.

Systems: Zendesk, Salesforce, Jira, Slack, internal administration tools and, where accessible, monitoring or logs.

### 3. Where time was lost or information repeated

- The practitioner manually summarized Zendesk history, customer details, reproduction steps, screenshots and troubleshooting into Jira.
- Engineering requested additional timestamps and a request ID.
- The customer had to reproduce the problem again.
- Support and Customer Success provided status updates while engineering investigated.
- Support could not access all monitoring or log information available to engineering.

No duration or handling-time estimate was supplied.

### 4. Customer or business consequence

- The customer waited longer for an answer and repeatedly requested updates.
- The issue was not a full outage, but it affected part of the customer's work.
- Customer Success also needed internal status.
- Support spent time communicating when no new technical information was available.

### 5. Current workaround

- Use a Jira escalation template.
- Copy the information engineering normally needs.
- Contact engineering through Slack for urgent issues.
- Ask Customer Success for unusual account context.
- Request new diagnostic evidence from the customer when initial information is insufficient.

## AI and risk

### 6. Current AI or automation

Zendesk provides ticket summaries, suggested replies, automatic classification and routing. An internal knowledge-search tool helps find documentation and prior answers.

Manual correction remains necessary because suggested replies can be generic, summaries require verification for escalations, and knowledge results may refer to old features or different configurations.

### 7. Actions requiring approval

- Permission or account-setting changes.
- Credits.
- Subscription changes.
- Disabling integrations.
- Promising an engineering fix date.

Drafting a response is acceptable; consequential customer-account actions should remain human-approved.

### 8. Is the native helpdesk sufficient?

Zendesk is sufficient for ordinary conversation management, assignment and status tracking. It does not remove the manual context and escalation work once information spreads across Zendesk, Salesforce, Jira, Slack and internal systems. The participant also warned that another tool would be harmful if it created another copy of the ticket to maintain.
