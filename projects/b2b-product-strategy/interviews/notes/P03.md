## Recent case

### 1. Last support-tool review

Three or four months before the interview, Support Operations requested a pilot of an AI support-analytics tool. The tool would analyse Zendesk conversations, identify recurring issues, generate summaries and suggest classifications. The request initially sounded read-only, but the integration also asked to write tags and internal notes into Zendesk.

### 2. Systems and people involved

`Support Ops request → Zendesk administrator review → application/integration review → security review → restricted pilot → procurement and legal review for annual purchase`

People: Support Operations manager, technology/application stakeholder, Zendesk administrator, security engineer, procurement and legal. The Salesforce application owner would need to approve any Salesforce write-back.

### 3. Where time or approval friction appeared

- The default integration requested broader access than the pilot required.
- The tool asked to read tickets, users, organisations, comments and attachments.
- It also requested write access for tags and internal notes even though analysis and classification were the initial goals.
- Attachments could contain credentials, logs, screenshots or customer data.
- The team needed clarity about vendor data retention.
- Exact OAuth scopes were not recalled and were not inferred.

### 4. Outcome and consequence

The team approved a restricted pilot rather than the vendor's default integration:

- Only two Zendesk queues were connected.
- Write-back was disabled.
- Tickets came from two product areas.
- The pilot lasted approximately one month.
- Support Operations liked the analytics but did not consider write-back mature enough for automatic use.

No quantified productivity, quality or financial outcome was supplied.

### 5. Current operating pattern

Zendesk handles routing, SLA triggers, tagging and macros. Workato workflows synchronize selected Zendesk and Salesforce fields and notify account teams. AI use is limited to agent-assist summaries, response drafting and some classification, with human review.

## AI and risk

### 6. Current AI or automation

Agent-assist summaries and drafts are available. Some teams use classification, but the participant did not describe it as fully reliable. Normal suggested replies require the agent to press Send.

### 7. Actions requiring approval

- Refunds.
- Customer-entitlement or account changes.
- Contract-related updates.
- Renewal-status or account-owner changes in Salesforce.
- Customer-facing messages or downstream workflows with material consequences.

The participant described application-specific approval rather than a single company-wide AI-action matrix.

### 8. Read-only versus read/write access

Read-only access can still expose sensitive production support data. Write access adds error-recovery and system-owner approval requirements. Low-consequence tags may be correctable, while customer messages, Salesforce records, entitlements and downstream actions require stronger review, ownership and rollback.

## Buying and pilot

### 9. Purchase and approval path

- Trigger: Request to connect an AI analytics tool to production Zendesk.
- Business owner/champion: Support Operations manager.
- Application owner: Zendesk administrator; Salesforce owner would govern Salesforce writes.
- Security: Security engineer reviewed the vendor and connection.
- Commercial approval: Procurement and legal joined before an annual subscription.
- Economic buyer and budget owner: Not identified.

### 10. Limited-pilot requirements

- A named business owner.
- Clear system, data and vendor-processing scope.
- Ability to constrain access by queue, team or dataset.
- Read-only access where write-back is not required.
- A short pilot with a limited production population.
- Lightweight security review proportional to risk.
- Clear vendor answers on data use and retention.
- Sufficient expected value to justify connecting another production application.
