# Customer Interview Execution Guide

## Objective

Determine whether support-led B2B companies repeatedly face one important cross-system support workflow and whether they would consider a separate product to solve it.

The interviews should discover real behaviour. They should not ask participants to approve the proposed Customer Operations Platform.

## Sample plan

Start with eight participants. Use the remaining four slots only if the evidence is inconsistent or an important role is missing.

| Participant type | Initial target | What they reveal |
|---|---:|---|
| Frontline support or customer success | 3 | Recent workflow, friction and workaround |
| Support or customer-success leaders | 3 | Operational impact, metrics and priorities |
| Support operations, CRM administrator, IT or buyer | 2 | Integration, risk, buying and pilot requirements |

Prefer participants from B2B SaaS or technology-enabled services companies with an established helpdesk and CRM. Sample across company size and support maturity instead of recruiting eight similar contacts.

## Execution sequence

1. Recruit the first three participants.
2. Book a 25 to 30 minute call.
3. Copy `interview-note-template.md` to `notes/P01.md`, replacing the participant ID as needed.
4. Ask for permission before recording. Written notes are sufficient when recording is declined.
5. Reconstruct one recent difficult support case before discussing a possible product.
6. Update `Interview_Research_Tracker.xlsx` immediately after the call.
7. Review patterns after interviews 3, 6 and 8.
8. Continue toward 12 only when themes remain contradictory or a role is missing.

## Evidence rules

- Use participant IDs such as `P01`. Do not store names or email addresses in Git.
- Do not store customer names, ticket IDs, account details, credentials or confidential screenshots.
- Keep exact quotes only when they are anonymized and necessary.
- Separate what the participant said from the researcher's interpretation.
- Preserve contradictions. Do not force every interview into the preferred strategy.
- Public reviews remain secondary research. Interview evidence must come from an actual conversation.

## Decision rule

Review the separate-layer strategy only when all four proposed gates are supported:

1. At least four participants independently mention the same workflow.
2. At least three describe measurable customer or business impact for that workflow.
3. At least three say their existing helpdesk or native AI does not adequately solve it.
4. At least two express strong pilot interest.

These thresholds organize judgment. They are not statistical proof. The underlying examples and limitations remain more important than the count alone.
