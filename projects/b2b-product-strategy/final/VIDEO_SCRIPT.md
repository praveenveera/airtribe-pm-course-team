# Airtribe B2B Product Strategy — Video Script

**Target length:** 4:20–4:40 at a calm pace

**Screen:** Keep the final PDF open and move through the cited pages.

**Before recording:** Do not describe the four interviews as a representative sample.

## 0:00–0:30 — Decision

Do not become another AI chatbot or replace the customer’s helpdesk. Land with one permission-aware workflow for difficult support cases: assemble context across the helpdesk and one system of record, prepare the next action, preserve the handoff, and write the outcome back. Start in agent-assist or shadow mode.

## 0:30–1:05 — Evidence and limitation

The research contains 91 public evidence records linked to 56 sources within a 65-source register. I also completed four anonymized interviews: three frontline practitioners and one application-approval stakeholder. Two are enterprise and two represent the proposed mid-market environment. The sample still lacks a support manager, confirmed economic buyer, lower-maturity team, price evidence, and proposed-product pilot commitment.

## 1:05–1:45 — Customer problem

One enterprise practitioner reported 20 to 25 minutes collecting context for each difficult case, with escalation details lost in Teams. The banking practitioner reported 15 minutes of duplicate logging and four-to-six-hour approval waits. A mid-market frontline practitioner manually reconstructed Zendesk information in Jira during an engineering escalation. A mid-market application stakeholder approved an AI analytics pilot only after limiting it to two Zendesk queues and disabling write-back.

The problem is fragmented information and approvals during difficult cases, causing search, duplicate entry, weak handoffs and approval delay. Complex-escalation context and handoff leads with two mentions; it is not yet validated.

## 1:45–2:25 — Market and competition

The adjacent market is large and support work is frequent, but market definitions vary, so I do not combine headline estimates into one TAM. AI adoption is rising while deployment maturity, data readiness and governance remain uneven.

Intercom, Zendesk, Freshdesk and Salesforce already provide AI, integrations, actions and governance. The testable differentiation is workflow completeness: permissioned context, visible evidence, safe approval, durable handoff, reliable write-back, faster deployment and predictable cost.

## 2:25–3:20 — Product and roadmap

The workflow has six steps: detect, resolve identity, assemble context, recommend, approve or hand off, and write back. The MVP covers one helpdesk, one system of record and one workflow. It excludes helpdesk replacement, open-ended autonomous actions and regulated approval decisions.

First prove one workflow. By month twelve, repeat it across common stacks using reusable connector and permission contracts. In year two, add adjacent workflows for the same team. Expand into selected Customer Operations workflows only when deployment is repeatable without services-heavy customization.

## 3:20–4:05 — Moat, GTM, and metrics

The moat is not the model or connector catalogue. Candidate moats are workflow evaluation data, reliable action contracts, a correction and reopen learning loop, and faster repeatable deployment.

The working buyer remains a Head or VP of Support or Customer Operations. One interview showed Support Operations as business owner, with application owners, security, procurement and legal in approval. The economic buyer remains unknown. Land with a 30-to-60-day limited-queue pilot. Measure context-collection minutes, guarded by reopens, unauthorized actions, audit completeness and customer harm.

## 4:05–4:35 — Close

All four decision gates remain unmet: the leading workflow has two mentions against four; quantified impact is one against three; native-tool insufficiency is two against three; strong proposed-product pilot interest is zero against two. Proceed only as a bounded assist-first hypothesis and validate it with managers, economic buyers and a controlled pilot.
