## Slide 1 — Find My Ride

Hi, I'm Praveen. This is Find My Ride. It looks at the moment when a driver is nearby, but the rider and driver still can't find each other. I wanted to know why that breaks, and what Uber should test first.

## Slide 2 — What we actually researched

I couldn't complete ten live interviews in time, so I built an online survey instead — English, Telugu, and Hindi, with separate rider and driver questions. After removing one duplicate, I had sixty-two responses: fifty-eight riders, four drivers — reaching further than ten interviews would. The tradeoff: no live follow-up on a surprising answer.

## Slide 3 — Market and alternatives

I also checked how other services handle this. Uber already guides Hyderabad airport riders to a pickup zone — this isn't a no-guidance problem. Grab, Lyft, and Waymo use meeting points and clear walking guidance. These prove the pattern, not local demand. The gap is guidance when the pin alone isn't enough.

## Slide 4 — Market, segments and trends

I couldn't find reliable data for an exact market size, so I'm not guessing a number. Instead, I show the method, and where demand concentrates: airports, gated communities, malls, offices, and stations.

## Slide 5 — Research goal and problem frame

My goal was simple: understand what breaks during a difficult pickup, and how people recover today. The rider is the main user; the driver is affected too. Success means fewer calls and a faster start.

## Slide 6 — Five-W problem framing

Before writing the problem statement, I checked five questions. Who: a rider at a complex pickup, with the driver also affected. What: no shared meeting point. Where: airports, gated homes, malls, offices, stations. When: as the driver arrives. Why: the extra coordination creates delay.

## Slide 7 — Problem statement

The final statement: at complex locations, a correct pin can still fail to create one shared meeting point. That causes calls, waiting, and delay. It doesn't apply to every ride. Rider evidence is broad; driver-side validation is still the open gap.

## Slide 8 — Key interview questions

These are my main survey questions: what happened, where, what the app showed, when confusion started, what people did next, and what actually helped. Drivers were asked what stopped them from reaching the rider. All online, no live follow-up.

## Slide 9 — Top insights: headline numbers

The problem is real, but not universal. Twenty-four of fifty-one recent car users reported difficulty. Of twenty-eight problem cases, twenty-three said it happened before. Twenty said calling or messaging helped; six said nothing helped. This describes my sample only, not all of Hyderabad.

## Slide 10 — Top insights: locations and recovery

Most difficult pickups happened at airports, gated communities, malls, offices, and stations. Calling was the top fix, then waiting or moving. Riders and drivers mostly solve the last step themselves. Full insights are in the appendix.

## Slide 11 — Strategy implications

My recommendation: help the rider and driver agree on one clear meeting point when a pickup gets hard. I'd test in order — a structured meeting note first, then help for a stuck pickup, then venue guidance. A test order, not a roadmap.

## Slide 12 — First priority: structured meeting note

The first test is a short note before the driver arrives — a gate, landmark, or waiting spot. I'd measure whether it cuts calls, waiting, and cancellations. It should only show up where it's useful.

## Slide 13 — Second and third priorities

The second idea is guided recovery — if they can't connect, the app suggests confirming the meeting point or calling. The third is verified guidance for a few venues, though that needs regular updates or it creates more confusion.

## Slide 14 — Recommendation and next steps

My recommendation is to test the meeting note first — the smallest change that addresses what I found. The research has real gaps: driver evidence is thin, only four responses, and the sample doesn't represent Hyderabad. Next, moderated interviews, then testing with actual pickup-delay data. Everything else is in the appendix. Thank you.
