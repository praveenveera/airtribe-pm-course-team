## Cover — Title

This is Find My Ride — a look at why, after you book an Uber, you and the driver still sometimes can't find each other. It's built on 62 real survey responses from Hyderabad and a few comparison cities, not guesses.

## Section 1 — Research Goal

The question was simple: when a pickup goes wrong, why does it go wrong, and what do people actually do about it? I didn't invent a metric to judge this by — the closest thing Uber could check today, without any new research, is the gap between the app saying "driver has arrived" and the trip actually starting. I don't have that number. But Uber does, or could pull it in a day.

## Section 2 — What the evidence actually showed

Here's the headline: this isn't everyone's problem. Only about half of recent riders reported real difficulty. But for that half, it wasn't a one-off — 23 of 28 said it had happened before, clustered in specific places: airports, gated communities, malls, office campuses — big or controlled spaces where the map pin and the real world don't line up.

The clearest single finding: the app can say "driver arrived" while you're still nowhere near each other. One rider told us it took almost ten minutes after that message. People fall back on calling and texting, and it usually works. But for six people here, nothing worked at all — no help, no way to escalate. That's the real gap: not that communication is missing, but that when it fails, there's no floor under you.

## Section 3 — Strategy implications

So here's the direction: focus on making that fallback — the calling, the texting, the waiting — faster and less effortful, at the specific places where it keeps happening. Not a redesign of the pin. Not an airport-only fix, because airport isn't even a majority of the cases.

One honest gap before I go further: this data is almost entirely riders. Only 4 of 62 responses came from drivers. Every time a driver circles a block looking for someone, they're losing paid time — and I don't have evidence yet on what that does to them, whether they get frustrated with certain locations, whether it affects whether they'll take that kind of trip again. That's not a footnote — it's the single biggest thing I'd want answered before anyone commits real money to this.

## Section 4 — The three ideas

Three ideas, in the order I'd build them.

One: a simple structured note, exchanged a few minutes before the driver arrives — landmark, which gate, where to stand. One rider in our survey already does this by hand, unprompted, every time. Lyft already ships almost exactly this feature, so this isn't a bet on something unproven — it's catching up to a competitor's already-validated idea.

Two: a guaranteed next step when things stall. Right now, if calling and texting don't work, you either wait indefinitely or cancel. This adds something in between — a guided way to reconnect, or in the worst case, reassignment. Worth saying clearly: reassignment means the original driver loses that time for nothing, so this needs a real compensation answer, not just a nicer screen for the rider.

Three: for the handful of places where this keeps happening, invest once in a shared note every future rider and driver can pull from, instead of everyone rediscovering the same friction. This one's the least proven of the three — we only checked venue rules from public sources, not by visiting — so treat it as a hypothesis, not a locked plan.

## Section 5 — Close, and what I'd honest about

I want to be upfront about the limits. This is a convenience sample — people from my own network, not a random sample of Hyderabad. Zero Hindi responses came in, even though we tried. And the group least able to recover from a bad pickup on their own — someone who doesn't read the map easily, or doesn't speak the app's language — is probably underrepresented here, not overrepresented.

So this isn't a "we're sure, build it" pitch. It's: here's a real, evidence-backed pattern, here's the one number Uber could check tomorrow to validate it, and here's the one research gap — the driver side — that needs filling before I'd bet more on this.
