# Uber Find My Ride — v3 (Hybrid of v1 and v2)

**Status:** v19 deck rebuilt (26 slides: 14 presented + 12 appendix) with explicit market-evidence and experiment-readiness coverage; video recording/link still pending
**Snapshot:** 19 September 2026
**What this is:** A hybrid that keeps v1's three distinct, assignment-literal product ideas, while carrying forward every strategic sharpening from the v2 senior-PM reframe. Nothing is deleted from either version.

## The decision this hybrid makes

v2 restructured Section 4 into two ideas: it merged the original per-trip note and venue-specific note into one phased bet, and used the freed slot to propose closing the driver-evidence gap directly. That's a stronger strategic narrative, but it trades away a concrete, assignment-literal "third idea" for a research ask — a risk against a rubric that likely expects three distinct product ideas.

v3 resolves that tradeoff by keeping three distinct ideas while correcting their claim strength: structured pre-arrival note, guided stalled-pickup recovery, and venue-specific coordination information.

- Idea 1 gets the Lyft "Pickup Notes" competitive precedent added.
- Idea 2 starts with guided reconnect actions; reassignment is only a later test for unresolved cases because someone must absorb the driver's lost time.
- Idea 3 gets an explicit "unverified, confirm before scaling" flag, since it depends on venue rules that were only desk-validated, not field-verified.
- Section 3 opens with v2's driver-side marketplace-economics framing, named as the leading risk to the whole recommendation.
- Section 5 carries v2's instrumentable-metric ask (arrived→trip-start dwell time) and the sample-skew/accessibility risk, both stated as the single highest-value next step — ahead of building further on any of the three ideas.

## What changed in v6

Slide 3 now states the problem frame before the market and solution sections. It names the
primary rider, the driver as connected stakeholder, a working JTBD, the target segment, and the
assumptions that still need validation. Slides 9–11 and appendix slide 20 now label the test
sequence explicitly as P0, P1, and P2. The deck does not fabricate a numeric TAM/SAM/SOM or add
Porter's Five Forces because neither would improve the decision with the available evidence.

## What changed in v8

The main presentation now maps visibly to the supplied template: `[1] Research Goal`, `[1.1]
Key Interview Questions`, `[2] Top 10 Most Relevant Insights`, `[3] Strategy Implications`, and
`[4] Research-Led Product Ideas`. The questions are labelled honestly as interview prompts
adapted for the asynchronous online instrument. Market and competitor research remains before
the template sections because it is a separate Step 1 requirement. The complete ten insights,
evidence-strength labels, limitations, and source traceability remain in the appendix.

## What changed in v9

The presenter notes on slides 1–12 were rewritten in simple, spoken PM language. Each note is a
complete word-for-word narration rather than a set of talking points. Technical phrases such as
"instrument", "dwell time", and "directional evidence" were replaced with plain explanations.
The 672-word script retains the research-method disclosure, problem statement, evidence limits,
prioritization, recommendation, and next steps while remaining suitable for a video under five minutes.

## What changed in v14

Appendix slide 15 adds an editable problem-framing quality check adapted from the supplied visual.
It places the current work in the evidence-led, problem-first quadrant while distinguishing current
directional validation from the stronger validation expected after moderated interviews and pickup
event data. The slide now breaks down the current user and setting, observed problem, evidence base,
success signal, and remaining gaps. Slide 5 narration points to the appendix check without adding
another narrated slide. The 673-word script keeps a safer recording-time buffer.

## What changed in v15

Presented slide 6 now makes the problem statement unmistakable. It follows the supplied four-part
format: specific user, clear pain, visible impact, and defined scope. The final statement focuses on
Hyderabad four-wheel riders at complex pickup locations, the lack of one shared workable meeting
point, the resulting calls, waiting, movement, and delay, and the value of improving pickup
reliability within driver access and stopping constraints. The slide also carries the evidence
boundary: the survey is directional, while moderated interviews and stronger driver validation
remain pending.

The former presented slides 6–12 shift to 7–13, the appendix starts at slide 14, and the expanded
problem-framing matrix is now slide 16. The 658-word speaker script is embedded in notes on all 13
presented slides and is approximately 4:32 at 145 words per minute.

## What changed in v17

Presented slide 6 now applies the 5W problem-framing check before the formal problem statement:
who is affected, what breaks, where it happens, when it becomes visible, and why it matters. The
slide uses only supported context and keeps root causes, driver impact, and venue constraints as
open validation needs. The final problem statement moves to slide 7, and the remaining presented
flow shifts to slides 8–14. The appendix starts at slide 15, the expanded framing matrix is slide 17,

## What changed in v19

Appendix slide 23 now separates supported market signals from inputs that are not available. It
covers demand and segments, the TAM/SAM/SOM framework, growth and CAGR, competitive evidence,
willingness to pay, saturation, and the correct use of proxies. It does not invent market-size or
growth values.

Appendix slide 24 adds the experiment-readiness check: the inputs needed before choosing an A/B
sample size, the P0-first MVP rule, the driver-side adoption blocker, and the demand,
discoverability, outcome, retention, and competition signals to monitor. Pricing power is labelled
as out of scope for the first reliability test. The limitations slide and source/video gate move to
slides 25 and 26. The narrated section remains slides 1–14 with the same 700-word script, so the
recording plan and timing do not change.

## What changed in v5

Slide 2 no longer says the interview requirement "was not met". It states the method choice,
the verified qualitative depth (30 open-ended responses, 11 extended accounts, 3 complete

appendix slide 14 lists the ten key research questions required by template section 1.1. Four
precision corrections were applied to the evidence slides.
