# Video script — Nykaa UX Analysis (target ~4:35, hard cap 5:00)

## Setup

- **Recording:** screenshare + webcam together the whole time (Zoom, Loom, and Meet all do this as a picture-in-picture bubble over your shared screen — you don't need to switch between "face" and "screen," both are on throughout). Airtribe's requirement is that you're visible, which the webcam bubble satisfies without you doing anything extra.
- **Share the deck:** `Nykaa-UX-Analysis.pptx`, in presentation/slideshow mode. If your software supports a presenter view (PowerPoint, Keynote), use it — you see these speaker notes on your own screen, the viewer only sees the slide. Google Slides' presenter view works the same way.
- **You are narrating slides 1–8 only.** That's the whole 5 minutes. Slides 9–18 are the appendix — full journey detail, real screenshots, the complete recommendations table, limitations. Don't click into them unless someone asks a question the appendix answers; then jump straight to the relevant slide (the deck's own agenda, slide 2, tells you which number).
- Speak at a normal pace — this script is ~700 words, comfortably under 5 minutes. Do one practice read and trim anything that runs long. **This is written the way you'd actually say it out loud — read it like you're talking to someone, not reading a list.** Don't read it word for word; use it as a track. **Bold** = the beat to hit.

## Slide 3 — The thesis — 0:30–1:00

"Here's my finding, in one line: Nykaa is strong at the start, when you're browsing and discovering, and strong at the end, in the bag and at checkout. Where it gets weak is the middle — turning a specific need into a confident choice.

The clearest example: I searched for a moisturizer for dry, sensitive skin, under 800 rupees. I got 696 results back, and the very first slot was an ad — for a set costing four to eight times my budget."

---

## Slide 4 — Five journeys — 1:00–2:20

"Let me walk through the five journeys quickly.

First, the moisturizer search: great product cards, and filters that genuinely work once you use them — but still 696 results, and ads as high as 6,600 rupees kept showing up on top, even after I'd filtered by price.

Second, a gift set: 91 curated results, felt like a real shop. The catch is, delivery date and gift wrap only show up deep in checkout, after I've already picked something.

Third, a first lipstick: one point four million reviews on a single product, and zero help actually picking a shade.

Fourth, I logged into an account with five years of order history to see if that changes anything. It doesn't — full order history, still no reorder button anywhere, home page identical to a stranger's. I even reran Journey A's exact search on that account to check — identical results, down to the review counts.

And fifth, just browsing, no goal at all — genuinely pleasant. Which is exactly the point: that's the one visitor Nykaa's home page is actually built for."

**How to say this one:** it's five short beats, not one long list to memorize — take a breath between each. "First... Second... Third... Fourth... And fifth..." is the scaffolding; the rest can drift from this exact wording without losing anything.

---

## Slide 5 — Three features — 2:20–2:55

"Now, three features, and my quick take on each.

Product cards — good. Everything I need to shortlist a product, in one glance.

Beauty filters — good idea, badly used. Skin type and concern match how people actually shop for this, and the filters work. But nothing ever suggests the obvious combination, and mobile guests hit a login wall before they can even try.

Logged-in home page — bad. Identical to logged out, even on an account with five years of history."

---

## Slide 6 — What two real people said — 2:55–3:35

"I spoke to my wife and my sister — one shops rarely, one shops often. Both are fully convinced buyers; my wife actually trusts Nykaa's products more than Amazon's. But neither of them complained about finding things. What they both brought up, completely unprompted, was what happens after checkout — delivery dates slipping with zero warning, and orders mislabelled 'cancelled' or 'returned' by the app.

Different frequency, same wall — and that's a layer I never reached in my own testing."

---

## Slide 7 — Recommendations — 3:35–4:25

"So if I owned this, here's what I'd prioritize.

Top of the list, P0 — a one-tap strip right after a concern search, narrowing by skin type, concern, and budget in one move. That alone could turn 696 results into about 40.

P1 — a 'Buy again' row for logged-in shoppers, plus one-tap reorder in Orders. Also P1 — let mobile guests filter without logging in. And one more P1, from the interviews, not my own testing — honest, proactive delivery status.

P2 — stop ranking way-over-budget ads above the best-fit product.

And one thing I'd deliberately not build — an AI chat assistant. The filters already exist and fit the category; the fix is using them at the right moment, not adding something new to learn."

---

## Slide 8 — Close — 4:25–4:45

"That's my analysis. Nykaa is a great shop window, and a weaker personal shopper. It's strong at discovering, strong at checkout — the gaps are the choice in the middle, and the trust in the follow-through. The fastest wins here are about using the data it already has, not adding a new surface. Thank you."
