# Project 4 — Secondary Research Pack

**Research date:** 22 September 2026
**Case:** How Zepto can increase Average Order Value without making urgent orders slower, more expensive, or less trusted.

**Phase boundary:** This pack intentionally excludes primary research. Interviews, surveys, and live user observation are deferred and must not be presented as current evidence.

## 1. Decision this research supports

The decision is not whether Zepto should add more catalogue. The decision is which basket-building opportunity is credible enough to test:

1. more relevant additions to an existing mission;
2. better value and pack-size guidance;
3. planned-basket or replenishment support; or
4. a different opportunity that secondary evidence reveals.

## 2. Evidence rules

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FACT** | Supported by a dated external report, official product surface, or company filing. |
| **COMPANY CLAIM** | Published by a platform or company; useful for positioning and scale, not independent validation. |
| **INFERENCE** | Reasoned interpretation of multiple sources. |
| **BEHAVIOURAL ASSUMPTION** | A user-behaviour belief that must be tested with Project 4 participants. |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | A product or business bet to validate through research or experiment. |
| **UNKNOWN** | Not answered by the secondary evidence. |

## 4. Market-size view

There is no single clean public number for Zepto’s addressable AOV opportunity. Use a layered market view instead:

| Layer | Evidence-supported view | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Broad online retail context | Redseer projects India online retail above $90B in CY2026. | Context only; this includes categories beyond grocery and quick commerce. |
| Faster-commerce opportunity | BCG/DTDC describes a $20B+ rapid-commerce GMV opportunity by 2030. | Directional category ceiling; rapid commerce is broader than Zepto’s exact model. |
| Zepto scale indicator | Zepto’s investor-relations site reports 2.3M+ orders per day in Q4FY26 and 119%+ order-volume CAGR between FY24–26. | Company-reported scale signal; useful for strategic importance, not independent market share or profitability proof. |
| AOV opportunity | Must be calculated from Zepto baseline orders: eligible orders × incremental contribution per order × retention effect. | This is the decision-grade model still missing from secondary research. |

### Recommended market-sizing formula

```text
Annual incremental value
= eligible annual orders
  × incremental net basket value per order
  × contribution margin after discounts and fulfilment
  × retained/repeat-order adjustment
```

Do not multiply a broad market-size number by an assumed Zepto share and present it as AOV opportunity. We need Zepto baseline data or an explicitly labelled scenario range.

## 6. Behavioural assumptions to test

| Assumption | Why it is plausible | What would disprove it | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urgent users stop once the immediate need is solved. | Quick commerce began as top-up shopping and users value speed. | Users routinely browse and add complementary items during urgent missions. | Recent-purchase story, item sequence, time pressure, checkout point. |
| Users reject additions that feel irrelevant or unnecessary. | Grocery value is contextual; generic promotions may create noise. | Users often add platform-suggested items regardless of mission. | Considered-but-rejected item and reason; recommendation recall. |
| Fees and thresholds can change basket size. | NIQ identifies fee avoidance as a basket-expansion behaviour. | Participants report no effect or add items only because they planned them anyway. | Cart total before/after threshold, fee visibility, item intent. |
| Offline/bulk channels win larger planned baskets through pack economics and trust. | Supermarkets and local stores support larger packs, physical inspection, and routine shopping. | Participants choose quick commerce even for large planned stock-ups because value is equivalent. | Channel comparison, pack size, price perception, freshness and trust evidence. |
| Replenishment is a safer expansion path than generic discovery. | Repeat needs are easier to predict and less intrusive than unrelated cross-sell. | Users dislike reminders or prefer to keep reorder flows narrow. | Prior purchase pattern, repeat behaviour, response to reminder concepts. |
| Higher AOV is valuable only if contribution and retention remain healthy. | Discounts and extra delivery effort can make gross AOV misleading. | Margin and repeat are unaffected across tested basket changes. | Baseline economics, margin, cancellations, refunds, repeat rate. |

## 7. Secondary-research conclusion

The market evidence supports a focused hypothesis, not a final product decision:

> Zepto’s most defensible AOV opportunity is to improve mission completion and useful basket expansion, especially where a user is already buying a recurring or complementary item. The product should make value, pack size, and total delivered cost clear, while preserving a fast exit for urgent orders.

The evidence does **not** justify:

- assuming every small order has expansion potential;
- treating assortment size as a product moat;
- using discounts as the default growth strategy;
- claiming competitor features increase AOV;
- or estimating Zepto’s AOV opportunity from total market size alone.
