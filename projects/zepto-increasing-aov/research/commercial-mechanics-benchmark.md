# Project 4 — Commercial Mechanics Benchmark

**Research date:** 22 September 2026
**Evidence type:** public terms, official product pages, and company material

## Why this matters

The AOV problem cannot be evaluated through product features alone. A larger basket may be caused by a useful addition, a fee threshold, a membership benefit, a promotion, or a forced cost. These mechanisms have different effects on margin, trust, and repeat use.

## Publicly documented mechanics

| Mechanic | Zepto | Blinkit | Swiggy Instamart | bbnow / BigBasket | Product implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery fee | Zepto terms say delivery fees and other charges are shown before checkout. | Blinkit terms allow delivery, platform, handling, convenience, small-cart, surge, rain, late-night, and other charges. | Swiggy terms describe free delivery for eligible Instamart orders under Swiggy One. | bbnow public pages advertise free delivery in some launch offers. | A fee can change the basket, but the effect is location-, account-, time-, and offer-dependent. |
| Small-cart / minimum-order logic | Zepto terms explicitly mention small-cart fees and Super Saver minimum-order thresholds. | Blinkit terms explicitly mention small-cart charges and cart-value-dependent service charges. | Instamart membership benefits are tied to minimum order amounts and plan conditions. | BigBasket FAQ has city-specific fee and minimum-order information. | Threshold mechanics should be benchmarked by actual city and account state, not assumed nationally. |
| Membership | Zepto Super Saver provides selected benefits subject to minimum-order thresholds and eligible products. | Public terms allow subscription/membership fees, but the exact benefit set is not established here. | Swiggy One provides eligible free delivery and discounts; benefits can vary by plan, city, and order amount. | No comparable national membership mechanic is established in this pass. | Membership can increase frequency and basket completion, but may shift economics from per-order fees to subscription cost. |
| Offers and vouchers | Zepto terms describe promotional vouchers and changing seller prices. | Blinkit terms describe credits, discounts, promo codes, vouchers, and discretionary changes. | Swiggy terms describe membership discounts and platform offers. | bbnow pages show top offers and launch promotions. | Record whether the offer creates genuine incremental value or merely changes payment timing/choice. |
| Delivery promise | Zepto terms say ETA is communicated and may vary; the company endeavours toward a 10-minute ETA. | Blinkit FAQ says more than 70% of deliveries are within 15 minutes; this is a company statement. | Swiggy corporate material reports an average Instamart delivery time of about 13 minutes in September 2025. | bbnow states 15–30 minutes on its current public surface and has older launch pages with 10–20 minutes. | Delivery speed is not directly comparable across dates, cities, and definitions. |
| Assortment signal | Zepto public surface says 7,000+ products and shows grocery, fresh, meat, pet care, beauty, and other categories. | Blinkit FAQ says 7,000+ products across grocery, fresh, bakery, meat/seafood, cosmetics, electronics, baby care, and more. | Swiggy material describes 30,000+ products and broad category coverage. | bbnow says 5,000+ essentials; BigBasket positions a wider catalogue and scheduled shopping. | Catalogue size does not prove local availability, discoverability, or AOV impact. |
| Price/pack economics | Zepto terms say seller prices may be MRP or discounted and can change; public pages show price and discount cues. | Public product surface and terms support price/offer comparison, but unit economics are not established. | Public material shows categories and offers; pack-level comparisons require location-specific observation. | BigBasket offers broader scheduled-shopping and pack-size context. | Unit price, pack size, and total delivered cost should be compared for matched baskets. |

## What the public data lets us say

1. All major platforms use a mix of speed, assortment, offers, and fees.
2. Fee and membership mechanics are conditional; there is no universal national threshold that can be safely used in the assignment.
3. Competitor terms support the hypothesis that basket value and delivery conditions interact.
4. Public sources do not show whether the user added an item because it was useful, because a threshold forced the decision, or because a promotion created impulse demand.

## Required comparison protocol later

For one chosen city and one consistent account state, record:

- first item and initial cart value;
- fee and ETA shown before adding anything;
- threshold or offer message;
- cart value and item count at threshold;
- total fees and discounts at checkout;
- unit price and pack size of matched items;
- whether the item is recommended, searched, bundled, or manually added;
- whether the basket still feels useful if the offer is removed.

This is a future live observation, not current secondary evidence.
