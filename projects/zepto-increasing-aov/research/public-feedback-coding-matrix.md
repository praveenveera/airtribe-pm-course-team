# Project 4 — Public Feedback Coding Matrix

**Research date:** 22 September 2026
**Evidence type:** structured synthesis of captured public sources
**Use:** secondary-research hypothesis generation; not a representative customer dataset

## How to read this matrix

The matrix codes recurring themes found across the public source register. A source count means that a theme appeared on multiple tracked source surfaces; it does **not** mean that the same percentage of customers experienced the issue. Individual reviews/comments have not been exhaustively sampled or coded.

Evidence labels:

- **Directional:** useful repeated public signal, but self-selected or uncontrolled.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible product interpretation that still needs validation.
- **Unknown:** cannot be established from current public data.

## Evidence-versus-judgment boundary

### Established from the current public pack

- These themes recur across more than one type of public source.
- Users publicly discuss the final payable basket, not only item prices.
- Freshness, availability, recovery, and locality are relevant to the shopping experience.
- Users compare quick-commerce apps with each other and with offline alternatives.

### Working product judgments

- Trust may be a prerequisite for profitable AOV growth.
- Inventory truth and fee clarity may be more valuable than generic recommendations.
- A recurring-basket workflow may be a stronger planned-shopping opportunity than full-order reorder.
- AOV strategy should be segmented by shopping mission and locality.

### Not established

- The prevalence of any issue.
- The national ranking of Zepto, Blinkit, Instamart, or BigBasket on quality or service.
- The causal effect of any feature, fee, recommendation, or discount on AOV.
- Whether users would accept a higher basket value if trust, price, or recovery improved.
- Whether any proposed opportunity improves contribution margin.
