# Project 4 — Product Teardown: Zepto and Competitors

## Teardown purpose

The primary product teardown is Zepto as the focal product. In this phase, the teardown is a **public-surface desk teardown**. Competitor teardowns are secondary benchmarks using the same public evidence lens. Live app observation and user testing are deferred with primary research.

## Journey lens

```text
Need appears → open product → discover/search → choose first item → find relevant additions → evaluate value/fees/pack size → checkout → learn/replenish next time
```

For every step, record: publicly visible product behaviour, evidence source, possible AOV mechanism, user risk, and the assumption that still needs primary validation.

## Primary teardown — Zepto

| Journey stage | Publicly observed / documented | AOV implication | Evidence status and next test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Zepto presents fast grocery delivery, category navigation, offers, and add-to-cart shopping. | Speed and low effort may support top-ups; category and offer surfaces may create discovery opportunities. | **Product fact.** Observe the same city/location with an urgent and planned mission. |
| Discovery | Public categories include global selections, pantry, munchies, beverages, bakery/biscuits, and other grocery missions. | Category breadth can expose complementary items, but breadth may also increase search effort. | **Product fact; impact unknown.** Test time to find the first item and the next relevant item. |
| First item | Product surface shows item price, discount, rating, and add-to-cart action. | Price and social proof may reduce decision friction; discount visibility may anchor users to deals. | **Product fact; causal effect unknown.** Compare full-price, discounted, and out-of-stock examples. |
| Basket building | Public evidence supports category browsing and adding products, but does not expose the respondent-specific recommendation logic. | Potential exists for complements, bundles, replenishment, and threshold guidance. | **Unknown.** Live teardown must capture home, search, product detail, cart, and checkout states. |
| Value evaluation | Zepto publicly promotes offers and free delivery, while exact fee/threshold varies by location and account state. | Thresholds may create useful additions or force low-value items; discounts can increase gross AOV while reducing margin. | **Partly observed.** Record cart totals before/after threshold and whether additions feel useful. |
| Checkout | Public surface confirms cart/add-to-cart behaviour but does not prove checkout design, fee transparency, or removal patterns for all locations. | Unexpected fees or unavailable items can cause abandonment or item removal. | **Unknown.** Complete a controlled checkout observation without placing an order unless authorised. |
| Repeat use | Zepto positions itself as a broad household platform. | Replenishment and habit could create larger recurring baskets if the product remembers useful routines. | **Company/product claim; user effect unknown.** Ask participants about repeat lists, history, and subscriptions. |

## Secondary teardown matrix

| Product | Entry and discovery | Basket-building angle to inspect | Main comparison question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blinkit | Fast hyperlocal entry; category breadth and 7,000+ product claim. | Does neighbourhood assortment make the next item more relevant? Are recommendations mission-aware? | Does Blinkit make a larger basket feel faster, or only make urgent top-ups faster? |
| Swiggy Instamart | Entry through the Swiggy ecosystem; grocery and broad non-grocery categories. | Does ecosystem/category breadth create useful cross-sell, or distract from the grocery mission? | Does the platform help users shift from one-item food/grocery needs to household baskets? |
| bbnow / BigBasket | Immediate delivery alongside broader BigBasket planned-shopping proposition. | Does freshness, value, pack size, and scheduled shopping support larger baskets? | Can the product bridge urgent top-ups and planned stock-up better than pure quick-commerce? |

## Deferred primary-observation protocol

When primary research resumes, use the same four missions for Zepto and each competitor where available:

1. One urgent top-up: milk or a missing ingredient.
2. One meal mission: ingredients for a planned meal.
3. One household replenishment: cleaning or personal-care products.
4. One stock-up mission: a multi-category weekly basket.

Capture screenshots or notes for entry modules, search ordering, product details, pack sizes, ratings, related products, cart recommendations, threshold messages, fees, out-of-stock behaviour, and checkout friction.

For this secondary-research phase, do not claim that the public surface is the same as the signed-in app experience. Do not call an element an AOV driver until user or behavioural evidence supports that interpretation.

## Interim teardown conclusion

Zepto appears to have the basic ingredients for basket expansion: broad category discovery, visible offers, product-level price cues, and a quick add-to-cart flow. The unresolved product question is quality of guidance, not absence of catalogue. A relevant addition that completes the mission may be valuable; a generic upsell may feel like noise or pressure.

The competitors provide three useful comparison angles: Blinkit’s neighbourhood assortment, Instamart’s ecosystem and cross-category missions, and bbnow/BigBasket’s bridge between instant convenience and planned value shopping. The final product recommendation must wait until primary evidence shows which pattern solves a real stopping point.
