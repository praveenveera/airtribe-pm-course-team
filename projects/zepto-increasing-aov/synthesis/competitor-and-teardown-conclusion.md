# Project 4 — Competitor and Product-Teardown Synthesis

## Current conclusion

The market does not show an empty feature category. Zepto, Blinkit, Instamart, and bbnow all compete through speed, assortment, categories, offers, and convenience. Therefore, “add more products,” “show more discounts,” or “add generic recommendations” is not yet a meaningful product strategy.

The working hypothesis is:

> Zepto can increase valuable AOV by helping users complete the specific mission they already started, then offering a small number of relevant, good-value additions or replenishment cues at the moment they are useful.

## Market-size and PM framing

The secondary research gives us a category anchor, not a verified Zepto TAM/SAM/SOM forecast:

- **TAM anchor:** BCG/DTDC describes a `$20B+` rapid-commerce GMV opportunity in India by 2030. This is broader than Zepto's exact operating model. [M01 source register](../research/source-register.md)
- **SAM scenario:** `$2.0B` base case, calculated as TAM × a 10% relevant-scope assumption. The scope could later be replaced with a verified Bengaluru, mission, category, or customer denominator.
- **SOM scenario:** `$100M` base case, calculated as SAM × a 5% attainable-share assumption. This is a planning scenario, not Zepto's actual market share.
- **AOV opportunity scenario:** `₹11.3B` annual gross opportunity, calculated from Zepto's reported 2.3M+ orders/day × 365 × a 30% eligible-order assumption × ₹45 incremental value/order assumption. It excludes discount, fulfilment, refunds, contribution margin, and retention effects.

These estimates are included to make the assumptions visible and testable. They should not be presented as customer findings, Zepto financial facts, or a forecast. The next evidence required is a comparable market denominator, Zepto baseline AOV/GMV, eligible-order segmentation, contribution margin, and primary research on whether the proposed additions are relevant and trusted.

## Evidence versus judgement

| Layer | Current finding |
|---|---|
| Product evidence | Direct competitors publicly show broad assortments, multiple shopping missions, offers, and fast delivery propositions. |
| Product evidence | Zepto publicly shows category discovery, offers, ratings, item prices, and add-to-cart behaviour. |
| Strategic inference | Breadth is becoming table stakes; relevance and mission completion are more promising comparison dimensions. |
| Hypothesis | Users stop baskets because the need is complete, the extra item is poor value, the pack size is wrong, discovery is difficult, or the order is no longer urgent. |
| Recommendation candidate | Test mission-aware additions, complementary bundles, and replenishment cues with clear value and easy dismissal. |
| Unknown | Which stopping reason dominates for recent Zepto orders and which recommendation pattern increases profitable, trusted basket value. |

## What Zepto should learn from each competitor

| Competitor / substitute | Useful pattern to study | Do not copy blindly |
|---|---|---|
| Blinkit | Local assortment relevance and breadth. | More assortment can increase choice burden and inventory complexity. |
| Instamart | Multiple missions and ecosystem discovery. | Cross-category expansion may distract from the immediate grocery job. |
| bbnow / BigBasket | Planned baskets, pack economics, freshness, and scheduled shopping. | A planned-shopping pattern may be too slow or heavy for urgent top-ups. |
| Supermarket / local store | Larger packs, physical trust, and familiar value. | Quick-commerce cannot assume it can win on every pack-size or price comparison. |
| Bulk / subscription | Replenishment routines and predictable value. | Recurring purchases require habit and reliability, not only reminders. |

## Candidate product bets to validate

### P0 — Mission-aware “complete this need” suggestions

Show a small number of complementary items based on the current mission, category, and basket—not a large generic carousel.

**Success signal:** more relevant additions with lower removal and dismissal frustration.
**Guardrails:** contribution margin, repeat rate, item removal, complaints, and perceived pressure.

### P1 — Value-aware basket guidance

Make pack-size economics, offers, fees, and thresholds easy to understand. Recommend an addition only when it is plausibly useful or helps the user reach a meaningful value point.

**Success signal:** increased basket value without a rise in discount dependency or post-checkout regret.

### P1 — Replenishment and planned-basket support

For users with recurring needs, test reminders, repeat baskets, or lightweight weekly stock-up suggestions.

**Success signal:** larger repeat baskets and higher retained contribution, not simply one-time promotion spikes.

## What not to conclude yet

- Do not claim that competitor assortment causes higher AOV.
- Do not treat company-reported product scale as user validation.
- Do not use non-Zepto responses as measured Zepto behaviour.
- Do not recommend logistics changes from a product teardown alone.
- Do not optimise for item count if the added items are unwanted, low-margin, or bought elsewhere anyway.
