# Five-minute walkthrough script

## 0:00–0:35 — Context and research question

“Our project asks how Zepto can increase AOV without making urgent orders feel slower or more expensive. We designed an independent academic study that includes Zepto users, competitor users, and people who prefer bulk, supermarket, local-market, or subscription shopping.”

## 0:35–1:25 — Research and segmentation

“The survey starts with one real purchase made within the last 30 days. It captures the shopping mission, first need, item count, spend range, categories, basket-building behaviour, considered-but-rejected items, and whether a fee or threshold changed that purchase. A Zepto user describes the real checkout decision. A non-Zepto user explains why the selected method fitted the purchase and whether Zepto was considered. Our working segments remain hypotheses until responses and follow-up conversations are collected.”

## 1:25–2:10 — Blockers and problem framing

“The likely blockers are completion of the urgent need, fear of unnecessary spend, irrelevant recommendations, stronger value in larger packs or offline channels, and delivery-fee or threshold effects. The expected expansion break is between first item and cart review, with another break at checkout when total price becomes visible. We will validate this with real purchase stories rather than treating these assumptions as findings.”

## 2:10–3:25 — Solutions

“We propose three solutions. First, a need-complete add-on shelf with only two or three complementary items. Second, transparent value packs and a household basket builder for planned shoppers. Third, optional reorder-plus prompts for familiar repeat orders. Each solution maps to a different blocker and segment.”

## 3:25–4:15 — Prioritization

“Our illustrative RICE prioritization puts the need-complete add-on shelf first because it reaches the broadest eligible audience, addresses the earliest expansion break, and can be tested with relatively low effort. The scores are planning estimates and will be replaced after baseline data and participant evidence.”

## 4:15–5:00 — Experiment and close

“We would measure AOV uplift, items per order, attach rate, multi-category orders, and cart expansion, while guarding conversion, delivery performance, complaints, refunds, and margin. The next step is to collect the survey and follow-up conversations, code the evidence, revise the segments and blockers, and then run a controlled experiment.”
